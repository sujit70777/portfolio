Diabetes Safety

Check your own medical instructions first.

  • Exercise can lower blood glucose, and some diabetes medicines can increase the risk of a low.
  • If you use insulin or medicines that can cause lows, follow your clinician's instructions about checking glucose before/after exercise and carrying fast-acting carbohydrate.
  • Low blood sugar is generally <70 mg/dL. Treat lows according to your diabetes plan; the CDC describes the 15-15 rule for many adults.
  • Check your feet regularly and stop if you develop an injury, blister, unusual swelling, or significant pain.

Leg / Ankle Rule

No “push through” on concerning symptoms.

If you have recurring leg/ankle symptoms, consider the seated bike instead of the elliptical when it is more comfortable. New or worsening one-sided swelling, severe leg pain, chest pain, fainting, or unusual shortness of breath needs prompt medical attention.

Sleep & Recovery

Daily target: 7.5–8 hours in bed.

A simple template is 11:00 PM → 6:30 AM. Shift it to fit your real schedule, but keep your wake and sleep times reasonably consistent.

Recovery is part of fat loss.

Don't turn six gym days into six maximum-effort days. Three strength days plus moderate cardio works better as a repeatable routine.

Safety & Progress

How hard should the weight feel?

Start with a load that lets you complete the planned reps with clean form and still feel you could do about 2–3 more good reps. No 1-rep-max testing.

When to progress: when you can complete all sets at the top of the rep range with good form, add the smallest available weight next time.
